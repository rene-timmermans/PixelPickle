PixelPickle: quick guide

PixelPickle is an easy-to-use photo editor that feels familiar right away: menus, tools and shortcuts are where you expect them.

The workspace

  • Menu bar and standard toolbar at the top: files, editing, adjustments, effects, layers and selections.
  • Tool options bar below it: the settings of the current tool, such as size, hardness and opacity. The tool's icon is at the front (hover over it to see its name). Use the Presets button to save and load your own settings.
  • Tools toolbar on the left. A button with a small arrow holds more tools: click the arrow to choose. Without an open image the toolbar is greyed out; with the first image the last used tool is active again.
  • Palettes on the right: Materials, Layers and History. Show or hide them via View > Palettes.
  • Right-click the image, a tab or a layer for a context menu.
  • Icons and buttons follow the accent color of your theme, on light and dark themes alike.

Opening, creating and saving images

  • File > New creates an empty image; File > Open opens existing files.
  • Saving with layers: .ora (OpenRaster, an open standard); vector layers stay editable in it too. Files from other photo editors in .pspimage can be opened and, if you like, saved that way as well. Common formats such as PNG, JPEG, TIFF, WebP and GIF save a merged image; your layers stay in PixelPickle.
  • Several images open at once: switch with Ctrl+Tab.

Tips:

  • The left mouse button paints with the foreground color, the right button with the background color.
  • Hold the space bar and drag to pan the image.
  • Selecting: with Freehand set to Point to point, Smart edge or Edge seeker, the image scrolls along when you reach the edge of the window. With the Add or Remove mode you extend a selection or cut a piece out of it, even when you click inside the selection.
  • Crop: drag a rectangle, adjust it with the squares on its corners and sides, and double-click inside it (or press Enter) to crop.
  • Perspective correction (with Straighten): drag the four corners of the frame along edges that should be straight, such as a building or a document photographed at an angle, and double-click inside the frame.
  • Clone: choose the source with the right mouse button and paint with the left. A circle follows at the source so you can see what is being cloned.
  • Scratch remover (with Clone): drag a narrow box over a scratch or hair; it is filled in from the pixels beside it.
  • Retouch brushes such as Sharpen, Soften, Lighten/Darken and Emboss work once per stroke; going back and forth does not build up. Turn on Continuous to build up the effect.
  • Art media: oil brush, chalk, pastel, crayon and colored pencil, each with its own stroke. Chalk, pastel, crayon and colored pencil have an adjustable paper grain; the oil brush mixes with the paint already there and runs out during a stroke.
  • Warping: the Warp brush pushes, expands, contracts or twirls pixels, and Iron out puts them back. Mesh warp lets you drag the points of a grid (Shift: whole row, Ctrl: whole column).
  • Text: click to create text, or click existing text to edit it again.

The pen and vector layers

  • The pen has four modes: Lines and polylines (straight lines, Shift for 45° steps), Point to point (Bézier curves) (click for a corner, hold and drag for a curve; click the first point to close), Freehand (Tracking makes the line smoother) and Edit (drag nodes and handles; Ctrl + drag from a node turns it into a curve).
  • The line stays editable until you press ✓ Apply or Enter; Esc discards it. Set line style, width, join and miter in the options bar.
  • With Create on vector (pen and shapes) what you draw becomes an editable object on a vector layer. To change it later, choose the pen in Edit mode and click the line or shape. You can then change the nodes, the colors and the line; Delete removes the object.
  • Vector layers stay vector when moving, mirroring, rotating and resizing, and in .ora. If you want to paint on one or apply an effect, PixelPickle first converts it to a raster layer.

Materials: color, gradient and pattern

  • The top box is the foreground (and the outline of shapes), the bottom box the background (and the fill). Click a box to open Material Properties.
  • There you choose a color, a gradient (linear, rectangular, sunburst or radial, with angle and repeats) or a pattern (with angle and scale; an open image or your own file works too). Dozens of gradients and patterns are included.
  • The small buttons under a box switch quickly between color, gradient and pattern; ⊘ makes it transparent (no outline or no fill). ⇅ swaps foreground and background.
  • Gradients and patterns work with the flood fill, brushes and shapes.

Layers

  • Create layers, vector layers, groups, masks and adjustment layers via the Layers menu or the buttons in the Layers palette.
  • Set opacity and blend mode per layer. An adjustment layer (for example Curves) changes everything below it without touching the pixels; a mask controls where.
  • Double-click a layer for its properties. On the Blend Ranges tab you decide per channel where the layer is visible, based on the layer itself and on what lies beneath it.

Adjustments and effects

  • Each dialog shows a before and after preview; you can zoom and pan the preview.
  • When a selection is active, the effect only works inside it and the dialog tells you so.
  • Save settings as a preset to recall them quickly later.
  • Effects > Effect Browser shows the effects side by side with a preview.

Undo

Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Alt+Z redoes. The History palette lists every step; click an earlier step to go back.

Preferences

  • File > Preferences > General Program Preferences: the language. PixelPickle is available in English, Dutch, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Russian, Chinese and Arabic.
  • File > Preferences > Autosave Settings: keeps changed images in a recovery folder at an adjustable interval. Your own files are never overwritten.
